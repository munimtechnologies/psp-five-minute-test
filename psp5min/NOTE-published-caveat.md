# Note (2026-11-26): the published paper's own caveat on its "sigma"

While checking for scoops on 2026-11-26 we found that the published ApJL version of Huang et al.
(ApJL 999 L4, DOI 10.3847/2041-8213/ae43ec) adds a caveat to its discussion that is not in arXiv
v1 (2511.10906v1, the only arXiv version). It says the trace PSD per bin is roughly chi-squared
distributed with six degrees of freedom, so the reported 5.8 and 6.2 sigma "should not be
interpreted as Gaussian tail probabilities, but simply as measures of how prominent the 3.1-3.2
mHz peak is relative to neighboring broadband fluctuations". The abstract still calls the peaks
"statistically significant (~6 sigma)". We read this through search-engine excerpts of the journal
page, because the journal site blocks our reader, and two independent excerpts agree.

This changes no grade in this repository. H1, H2c and H4 measure how often the statistic fires by
chance and what false-alarm probability the peaks have. The authors do not give those numbers, and
their caveat says the sigma is not one. Our article credits the caveat and does not suggest that the
paper presents the sigma as a tail probability.
